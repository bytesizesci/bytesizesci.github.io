One of my goals for 2025 is to write more. As written, this goal is lacking many qualities of a SMART goal (mnemonic for specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound). To make it smarter, I've created a few guidelines. My goal will be to post 24 times at a rough 2/month candence. This post counts for number one, leaving 23 to go. To stave off writer's block, I'll pen at least 23 questions or topics to use as prompts. The questions or topics will be more related to science or my research interests than my personal life and other interests. Let the brainstorming begin!

1. What supplements should you take when you (or your significant other) are trying to get pregnant? For males? For females?
2. What should you look for when adding a supplement to your ruotine?
3. Why do runners sometimes get an upset gut when they run long distances?
4. What cooking oil should I use for what?
5. Can adding a grocery store to a food dessert increase the number of whole foods people eat?
6. When is the best time to exercise?
7. Why is exercise healthy?
8. What does it mean when people say they have inflammation?
9. How often do people at different ranks (e.g., PhD students, postdocs, assistant professors, associate professors...) get imposter syndrome?
10. Should I buy a sauna?
11. If you teach people how to exercise and give them access to a gym, do they increase their activity levels?
12. What is your microbiome?
13. What are probiotics? What are prebiotics? Can babies take them?
14. What is a polygenic risk score (PGS)? Can they tell me about my risk for disease?
15. What is the NIH All of Us study? 
16. I want more data on me. What to do with it when you get it.
17. Does my research matter?
18. How can I convince my [person I care about] to exercise?
19. How much do postdocs get paid? What impacts it?
20. How much do faculty get paid? What impacts it?
21. What is fasting? Is it right for me?
22. What to do with your genetic data?
23. Is natural wine any better for you than wine with added sulfites?
